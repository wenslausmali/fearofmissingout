What if you are not actually behind?

What if the life you keep comparing yourself to was never supposed to be your life?

In Fear of Missing Out: When Comparison Steals Your Joy and Peace, Wenslaus Mali explores the quiet pressure that makes us feel like everyone else is moving faster, earning more, finding love sooner, and living a better life.

Through honest stories, Kenyan humor, and practical reflection, this book unpacks FOMO where it hurts the most.

Love

Life
When other lifestyles make yours feel inadequate.
Family
When expectations make you feel behind.
Purpose and Faith
When everyone seems to have found their calling except you.
Social Media
When watching others stops you from living your own.

But this book is not about giving up on ambition. It is about learning the difference between healthy ambition and comparison driven pressure.

And it introduces something different: JOMO, the Joy of Missing Out. The freedom to say maybe I do not need everything, maybe I do not need to be everywhere, maybe I do not need to become everyone, and maybe my life can be enough while it is still becoming.

From the 107 shillings M-Pesa balance to the slow matatu, from job applications and family expectations to social media and faith, this book reminds you that your journey does not have to look impressive to others to be meaningful to you.

Your Matatu may be slow. Your Matatu is yours. And it is moving.

Your life is not a race simply because everyone around you is running.
It can make you compare your ordinary Tuesday with someone else’s biggest announcement.
You are allowed to grow quietly.

From the Author’s Note

I did not write this book because I have figured life out.

I wrote it because, for a long time, I felt like everyone else had.

At one point, I had a Civil Engineering degree but no job. I had days when my M-Pesa balance was just 107.50 shillings. I spent a season arranging two hundred plastic chairs at events while wondering whether my education had taken me anywhere.

I also watched friends move forward while I remained in what felt like a waiting room.

And that waiting room became one of my greatest classrooms.

I began to realize that FOMO is not only about missing parties, trips or social media. Sometimes FOMO is the fear that you are missing your own life.

But I have also learned something important:

Being slow is not the same as being lost.

About the author

Wenslaus Mali

Wenslaus Mali is a young Kenyan writer, raised between village hills and Nairobi’s shantytown estate.

He holds a degree in Civil Engineering and has training in Computer Studies, Social Media Marketing and Social Media Management and Brand Content Strategy.
